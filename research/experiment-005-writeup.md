## 1. ABSTRACT

This technical note addresses the problem of computing exact 3D cross-sectional manifold meshes from the intersection of regular 4D polytopes with a 3D hyperplane defined by $a x + b y + c z + d w = e$. While existing 4D Gaussian splatting and time-varying graphics platforms approximate spatial slice representations through point sampling or localized falloff evaluation, this work implements a deterministic, cell-based 4D hyperplane slicing algorithm that computes explicit 3D boundary representation (B-rep) meshes. Evaluated across a unit tesseract ($[0,1]^4$), a 5-cell ($4$-simplex), and a 16-cell ($4$-orthoplex), the slicing pipeline yields 100% agreement with derived analytical reference sequences (including the main-diagonal tesseract topological transition $1 \to 4 \to 12 \to 6 \to 12 \to 4 \to 1$ and the 16-cell central slice cuboctahedron $N_v = 12$). Structural invariants—face coplanarity within $10^{-6}$, 3D convex half-space enclosure within $10^{-5}$, $2$-manifold watertight closure, and multiset edge-length metric congruence between $mesh(t)$ and $mesh(4-t)$ within $10^{-5}$—were validated across swept ranges. This work concerns classical computational geometry of convex polytopes in $\mathbb{R}^4$; no claim of a physical fourth spatial dimension is made.

## 2. BACKGROUND

Recent advances in high-dimensional scene representations—including 4D Gaussian Splatting (Wu et al., 2023), GL4D (Chu et al., 2024), Viviani4D, and benchmark platforms such as Open4D and Style4D-Bench—demonstrate the utility of 4D spatial and temporal primitives for dynamic graphics. However, the majority of neural and point-based 4D rendering pipelines visualize time-slices by interpolating 3D Gaussian centroids or evaluating scalar opacity decay along a temporal axis. These methods prioritize visual plausibility over topological and geometric exactness.

This work sits alongside these representations but focuses on a narrower computational geometry scope: constructing exact, closed 3D polyhedral meshes representing the exact intersection volume $P^4 \cap H^3$ of a convex 4D polytope $P^4 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ with an affine 3D hyperplane $H^3 \subset \mathbb{R}^4$. Unlike neural representations or real-world dynamic capture pipelines, this approach contains zero learned parameters, relies on deterministic linear algebra, and evaluates against closed-form analytical geometry.

## 3. METHOD

### 3.1 Hyperplane-to-Polytope Slicing Algorithm

The slicing algorithm processes a 4D boundary representation consisting of 4D vertex coordinates $V \subset \mathbb{R}^4$, a global edge list $E$, and a collection of 3D boundary cells $C$.

1. **Hyperplane Distance & Edge Crossing**:  
   Given a hyperplane $H: a x + b y + c z + d w = e$, the signed distance from a vertex $p \in \mathbb{R}^4$ to $H$ is:
   $$d(p) = a x + b y + c z + d w - e$$
   For an edge connecting $p_1$ and $p_2$, sign distance values $d_1 = d(p_1)$ and $d_2 = d(p_2)$ are evaluated against tolerance $\epsilon = 10^{-7}$:
   - If $|d_1| \le \epsilon$ and $|d_2| \le \epsilon$, the edge is classified as `ON_PLANE`.
   - If $|d_1| \le \epsilon$, $p_1$ is classified as a `VERTEX` intersection ($t = 0$).
   - If $|d_2| \le \epsilon$, $p_2$ is classified as a `VERTEX` intersection ($t = 1$).
   - If $d_1 \cdot d_2 > 0$, the edge does not cross $H$ (`NONE`).
   - If $d_1 \cdot d_2 < 0$, the exact intersection point $p_{int}$ is interpolated at parameter $t = \frac{-d_1}{d_2 - d_1}$:
     $$p_{int} = p_1 + t (p_2 - p_1)$$

2. **Cell-Based Polygon Winding & Mesh Assembly**:  
   For each 3D boundary cell $c \in C$, intersection points generated by its boundary face edges are collected and deduplicated within spatial threshold $\epsilon = 10^{-5}$. The resulting coplanar points are projected onto a local 2D orthonormal basis $(u_0, u_1)$ derived via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization around the cell centroid $\bar{p}$. Vertices are sorted by polar angle $\theta = \text{atan2}(y, x)$ to construct an ordered, convex polygon face. Faces from all cells are combined, deduplicated, and assembled into the global 3D cross-sectional mesh.

Implementation source: [`hyperplaneSlicer.ts`](file:///d:/4th%20dimension/4th-dimension-engine/src/math/hyperplaneSlicer.ts).

### 3.2 Target Polytopes & Analytical References

Three regular 4D polytopes were selected due to their known, closed-form analytical cross-section sequences:

- **Tesseract ($8$-cell / Hypercube)**: 16 vertices, 32 edges, 8 cubic cells ([`generateTesseractGeometry`](file:///d:/4th%20dimension/4th-dimension-engine/src/math/polytopeGeometry.ts#L27)).
- **5-cell ($4$-simplex)**: 5 vertices, 10 edges, 5 tetrahedral cells ([`generateSimplex5Geometry`](file:///d:/4th%20dimension/4th-dimension-engine/src/math/polytopeGeometry.ts#L117)).
- **16-cell ($4$-orthoplex)**: 8 vertices, 24 edges, 16 tetrahedral cells ([`generateOrthoplex16Geometry`](file:///d:/4th%20dimension/4th-dimension-engine/src/math/polytopeGeometry.ts#L165)).

#### Analytical Reference Sequences & Refinement Note
For a unit hypercube $[0,1]^4$ sliced along its main space diagonal by hyperplane $x + y + z + w = t$ for $t \in [0, 4]$:
- $t = 0.0$ and $t = 4.0$: Single vertex contact ($N_v = 1$).
- $0 < t < 1.0$: Tetrahedron ($N_v = 4$, $N_e = 6$, $N_f = 4$ triangles).
- $t = 1.0$: Transition boundary.
- $1.0 < t < 2.0$: **Truncated Tetrahedron** ($N_v = 12$, $N_e = 18$, $N_f = 8$, consisting of 4 equilateral triangles and 4 regular hexagons). *(Note: Initial exploration hypothesized a simpler 6-vertex cross-section; analytical derivation confirmed the 12-vertex truncated tetrahedron slice, eliminating prior naming ambiguity with hexagonal prisms).*
- $t = 2.0$: Regular Octahedron ($N_v = 6$, $N_e = 12$, $N_f = 8$ triangles, point of central symmetry).
- $2.0 < t < 3.0$: **Truncated Tetrahedron** ($N_v = 12$, $N_e = 18$, $N_f = 8$, 4 triangles and 4 hexagons).
- $3.0 < t < 4.0$: Tetrahedron ($N_v = 4$, $N_e = 6$, $N_f = 4$ triangles).

For the 16-cell, the central hyperplane slice $x + y + z + w = 0$ intersects 12 non-antipodal edge midpoints, yielding a **Cuboctahedron** ($N_v = 12$, $N_e = 24$, $N_f = 14$, consisting of 8 equilateral triangles and 6 squares).

### 3.3 Verification Methodology

The validation suite implements explicit mathematical checks:

- **Face Coplanarity (`verifyFaceCoplanarity`)**: Computes an orthonormal 2D plane basis $(u_1, u_2)$ in 4D space for each polygon face and asserts $\|P_k - \text{proj}_{u1,u2}(P_k)\| \le 10^{-6}$ for all face vertices.
- **3D Convexity (`verifyMeshConvexity`)**: Evaluates the 3D convex hull half-space test. For every face plane $n \cdot x = d$, signed distances to all mesh vertices $Q$ are measured; if vertices exist on both sides ($\min < -10^{-5}$ and $\max > 10^{-5}$), the check fails.
- **Metric Symmetry & Congruence (`verifyMeshCongruence`)**: Evaluates 4D Euclidean edge lengths for $mesh(t)$ and $mesh(4-t)$, asserting exact multiset equality $|L_1[k] - L_2[k]| \le 10^{-5}$.
- **Adversarial Testing**: Verification functions were tested against perturbed non-coplanar face inputs ($+0.5$ $Z$-offset) and concave vertex perturbations (inward dent point), confirming that invalid geometry is explicitly rejected.

## 5. LIMITATIONS

1. **Regular Polytopes Only**: Validation is currently restricted to regular 4D convex polytopes (tesseract, 5-cell, 16-cell). Slicing of irregular, non-convex, or imported real-world scanned 4D meshes has not been evaluated.
2. **Unverified Scalability & Infrastructure Gaps**: As documented in [`KNOWN_GAPS.md`](file:///d:/4th%20dimension/KNOWN_GAPS.md), two operational benchmarks remain open and unverified:
   - GPU WebGL2/WebGPU rendering performance at scale ($500,000$ primitives at $\ge 30$ FPS).
   - Backend concurrent user load capacity under $10,000$ simultaneous user bursts.
3. **No External Peer Review**: This write-up and implementation represent internal platform engineering validation and have not undergone independent academic peer review.
4. **No Physical Dimension Claim**: This work is strictly an exercise in computational linear algebra and polyhedral geometry in $\mathbb{R}^4$. It provides zero evidence or claim regarding physical extra dimensions.
